## Product debate

| Direction | User value | Differentiation | Trust | Build risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional content generator | Fast drafts | Low | Weak without source contract | Low | Useful formats, insufficient primary product |
| Exact Semrush workflow replacement | Proven owner workflow | High customer relevance | Strong when receipts and exact reports are preserved | Medium | Primary acceptance contract |
| General automation canvas | Broad flexibility | Low against mature workflow tools | Hard to reason about credentials and side effects | High | Do not recreate n8n editor |
| Shared research-to-content engine with workspace presets | Both commercial and client value | Evidence, product-policy and report continuity | Strong provenance and review | Medium | Chosen architecture |
| Skeptic: retain n8n | Minimal migration cost | No new product | Current runtime already familiar | Low initial, ongoing complexity | Retain workflow as benchmark; replace only with verified end-to-end behavior |

Chosen promise: a marketer can start from curated sources or an immediate trend document, configure deliverables, and obtain source-grounded reports, channel drafts and image assets with clear budgets and review states. General and Semrush workspaces share code but persist separate knowledge, programs, runs and asset libraries. These local workspaces are not authenticated enterprise tenant isolation.

Preserve dissent: a generic template selector cannot replace large-batch research. Full-cohort processing, exact evidence contracts, product eligibility, recoverable jobs and useful exports must ship with the polished interface. Generated images and text must be actual provider outputs, never canned examples shown as completed research.
